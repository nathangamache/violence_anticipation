# VA30: the violence-anticipation benchmark as implemented

Audience: anyone who trains or scores a model in this repo. This file documents the code in
`anticipation/data/` and `anticipation/eval/` and the committed files in `benchmark/`. It implements
[BASELINE.md](BASELINE.md) section 6; where the spec left a choice open, the choice made is stated here
(marked **Decision**). Version 2 (2026-10-14, after an independent review of version 1; the review log is
section 10). Numbers are read from the committed files.

---

## 0. TL;DR

* **Ground truth:** `benchmark/videos.csv` (7696 unique videos, 429 h) and `benchmark/events.json` (violent
  segments in seconds). XD-Violence test + train, all of UCF-Crime, and **UBI-Fights** (new in v2). Official onsets
  for the XD / UCF test splits, **HIVAU-70k onsets for the train splits**, per-frame labels for UBI-Fights. VA30
  evaluates 2129 violent videos (**551 eligible 30 s events** in 468 videos, 218 eligible 60 s events) against 4010
  clean negatives (304 h).
* **Folds:** `benchmark/folds_v2.json` (content sha256 `8ebdf6ec2e54...`), 5 grouped folds over 7604 videos. Groups
  merge XD franchises and near-duplicate copies (`benchmark/near_duplicates.json`), and every fold's validation slice
  is drawn independently. `folds_v1.json` is kept frozen but superseded (it predates UBI and leaked 41
  near-duplicate pairs across folds).
* **Evaluator:** `python -m anticipation.eval.va30 --scores <table> --out_dir <dir>` computes every metric of
  BASELINE 6.4 with 95 % cluster-bootstrap CIs and writes `metrics.json` + `report.md`.
* **Three shortcuts and how VA30 v2 handles them (section 8):**
  1. **Position + provenance.** Onsets sit early in their clips while clean negatives are long, and the source
     (CCTV / movie / YouTube) is visible in every frame. A label-free (source x time-since-start) model fitted on
     the training folds (`b_time_source_fit`) reaches far-window AUROC **0.86** and AUROC@30 **0.90**. Read every
     frame metric next to the **source x time matched** AUROC (0.50 for any such model by construction).
  2. **Position at the event level.** Raising one alarm at the start of every clip of a source "anticipates" every
     onset between 32 and 122 s. The Poisson random reference does not see this, so v1's claim that Success@30 is
     robust to position was wrong. v2 reports a **position null** (best Success@30 of position-only oracles at the
     same FA/h) and **Success@30 on late events** whose acceptance window excludes the clip start.
  3. **Detection memory.** 43 % of eligible events are not the first segment of their clip; their lead-in comes after
     violence the model has already seen. The headline frame metrics now use **pre-violence ticks only** (a perfect
     detector with memory, `b_memory`, scores 0.50 there and 0.72 on the spec-literal version).
* **HIVAU train onsets are not validated.** HIVAU's test entries are copies of the official annotations, so the
  cross-check only validates parsing. Every `metrics.json` therefore breaks results down by onset source; treat
  the **official-onset** breakdown as co-primary until the human spot-check (`anticipation.data.spotcheck`) is done.

| Curated YouTube (`yt`) | `anticipation_curation/videos/` (92) | **pseudo** (LSTM pseudo-labels; hold-out, never evaluated by default) |

Video-level classes: XD from the `_label_` codes (B1 fighting, B2 shooting, B4 riot, B5 abuse, B6 car accident,
G explosion, A normal); UCF from the folder / file prefix; UBI fight / normal.

**Violent classes (anticipation targets):** fighting, shooting, riot, abuse, assault, robbery, arrest.
**Interpersonal flag:** fighting, abuse, assault, riot, robbery, arrest (shooting is violent but not
interpersonal under this definition; `docs/codebase/data_and_labels.md` used B1/B2/B5 + UCF
Abuse/Arrest/Assault/Fighting/Robbery/Shooting).

`role` column (only the first two enter the default evaluation):

| role | meaning |
|---|---|
| `violent` | video-level class intersects the violent classes AND trusted violent onsets exist (official / hivau / ubi) |
| `clean_negative` | XD `_label_A`, UCF `Normal_Videos*` (all three normal folders), UBI normal |
| `other_anomaly` | anomaly classes that are not violence (car accident, explosion, arson, burglary, stealing, shoplifting, vandalism, road accident). Neither positives nor negatives. |
| `unlabelled` | violent class without trusted violent onsets: 20 UCF train videos HIVAU does not cover, 27 mixed-label XD clips whose segments HIVAU judges all non-violent, UBI `F_113_1_2_0_0` (no positive frame). Not negatives. |
| `duplicate` | identical re-encode of another catalog video (`duplicate_of`), section 2.2 |
| `contaminated` | clean clip whose source-film span overlaps annotated violence of another clip (1 clip) |
| `holdout` | curated YouTube (pseudo onsets only) |

**Decision (mixed-label XD clips), changed in v2:** a clip whose codes include a violent and a non-violent class
(e.g. `B2-G`, shooting + explosion) is split per segment with HIVAU's per-event judgement
(`events_summary_split[i].judgement`, aligned 1:1 with HIVAU's events; for official test segments the HIVAU test
event with the same start). `annotations.classify_judgement` maps the free LLM text to violent / other / none.
Segments judged violent stay onsets; segments judged explosion, crash or "no anomaly" become **`other_segments`**:
excluded time (masked with a 10 s aftermath, never positive, negative or false alarm) that also ends a clear
lead-in. 58 violent clips have 114 excluded segments; 27 clips lose all violent segments and become `unlabelled`.
v1 counted every segment as violent and claimed HIVAU could not tell them apart; it can.

### 2.2 Deduplication
* Same stem in several UCF folders (`videos/`, `Anomaly-Videos/<cat>/`, `Normal_Videos_for_Event_Recognition/`,
  `Testing_Normal_Videos/`): one row, canonical path by folder priority `videos/` > `Anomaly-Videos/` >
  `Testing_Normal_Videos/` > `Normal_Videos_for_Event_Recognition/` > `Training-Normal-Videos/`. The other paths
  are in `aliases`. 8795 files -> 7745 unique names.
* Byte-identical content under different names (sha1 over size + first and last MiB): 49 more removed
  -> **7696 unique videos** (20 UCF normal pairs, `ucf/Assault050_x264` = `ucf/Robbery138_x264`, 27 UBI normal
  copies, and `xd/v=qQCTvJq83_s__#1_label_B1-0-0` = `..._label_A`, kept as violent with a `label conflict` note).
* **Near-duplicates (new in v2, `anticipation/data/near_dups.py`):** 16x12 grey thumbnails at 1 fps for the first
  60 s plus 9 frames at 5 %..95 % of every video (cached in `data/annotations/_cache/fingerprints/`, about 20 min for
  the first pass with 12 workers). Rules:
  * `identical`: durations within max(1 s, 1 %), the 9 fraction-aligned frames agree (median correlation >= 0.97,
    median absolute difference <= 3 grey levels). 30 pairs. The non-canonical copy becomes role **`duplicate`**
    (canonical = violent with official onsets first, then other roles, then the test split, then the id). 28
    videos, e.g. `ucf/Arrest009_x264` (HIVAU onset 17.6 s, class arrest) is a re-encode of `ucf/Shooting015_x264`
    (official onset 28.5 s, class shooting) and is dropped in favour of it; `xd/v=rAfhOSNp16g` = `xd/v=xLFbqPzSI2Y`.
  * `similar`: same, but MAD > 3 (same static camera with a brightness shift, or filmed twice). 28 pairs, grouped only.
  * `overlap`: candidates from locality-sensitive hashing of the head frames, verified at the best integer-second
    lag (>= max(6, 60 % of the overlapping frames) with correlation >= 0.95 and MAD <= 6). 35 pairs, grouped only.
    Includes **cross-dataset copies**: UBI-Fights `F_108`, `F_15`, `F_23` (and `F_78`, similar) are XD-Violence
    YouTube clips; UCF `Normal_Videos307` (5.8 h) and `Normal_Videos308` (9.0 h) share their first minutes.
  * All 93 pairs (73 clusters) are merged into one **`split_group`** (section 4). The 12 pairs the reviewer found by
    hand are all detected. Limits: overlaps that start after the first minute of both clips and differently cropped
    copies are missed, so this is a lower bound.

### 2.3 Cross-clip masks for XD clips of the same film (new in v2)
XD clip names carry their position in the source film (`__#HH-MM-SS_HH-MM-SS`, `annotations.parse_xd_source_span`).
`catalog.source_masks` maps every violent segment of a clip onto the other clips of the same group that do not
annotate that violence themselves:
* its violence + 10 s aftermath -> `mask_spans` (excluded, and it ends a clear lead-in);
* its 120 s pre-onset window -> `mask_spans` for clean clips (their genuine pre-onset tail is neither a negative
  nor a false alarm), `soft_mask_spans` for violent clips (excluded only where the clip itself labels the ticks as
  far from its own violence; never ends its own lead-in).
121 clean clips lose 1.7 h in total (e.g. `Casino.Royale.2006__#00-00-37_00-01-52_label_A` ends 0.8 s before the
next clip's fight); `Taken.3.2014__#01-22-23_01-23-15_label_A`, which lies inside a violent clip's span, becomes
`contaminated`. 434 XD clips carry some mask.

## 3. Tick labels (`anticipation/data/labels.py`)

`tick_labels(duration, segments, other_segments=None, mask_spans=None, soft_mask_spans=None)` returns arrays over
ticks `t = 1..floor(duration)` (the feature clock). `labels_for_video(video_id, catalog_row, events)` reads them
from a catalog row and the events.json dict (trainers should use it):

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| `next_onset`, `tte`, `next_event` | first violent segment start strictly after t; `tte = next_onset - t` (inf if none); index of that segment |
| `in_violence` | `s <= t <= e` for a violent segment |
| `aftermath` | `e < t <= e + 10` (and not in violence) |
| `excluded` | ticks in other-anomaly segments (+ 10 s aftermath), `mask_spans` or `soft_mask_spans` |
| `masked` | in_violence or aftermath or excluded; never used by anticipation metrics or losses |
| `pre_violence` | `t <` the first annotated segment of any kind (all True if none) |
| `y[H]` | `0 < tte <= H`, H in 5, 10, 20, 30, 60 |
| `censored[H]` | no onset in (t, t + H] and `t + H > duration` (the future is unobserved) |
| `valid[H]` | `~masked & ~censored[H]` |
| `hazard_target [T, 6]` | bins (0,5], (5,10], (10,20], (20,30], (30,45], (45,60]: 1 in the event bin, 0 before it, -1 after it; with no onset within 60 s: 0 in every bin whose upper edge <= remaining footage, -1 after (right-censoring); -1 everywhere on masked ticks. `hazard_to_cdf(h)` gives P(onset within edge_j) = 1 - prod(1 - h_i). |

`event_table(segments, blockers=blocker_spans(other_segments, mask_spans))`: per segment `onset, end, clear_start,
leadin, first_tick, max_lead, eligible30, eligible60`. `clear_start = 0` for the first segment and `previous end +
10` otherwise, raised to the end of any blocker (excluded span that ends a lead-in) starting before the onset.

**Decision (eligibility on the 1 Hz clock), changed in v2:** an onset is eligible for lead L if `leadin >= L` AND
`max_lead = onset - max(2, ceil(clear_start)) >= L`. Tick 2 is the earliest tick at which the default 2-of-3
smoothing can raise an alarm, so a first onset in [30, 32) s, or a later onset whose fractional clear start leaves
less than 30 s on the clock, can never reach Success@30. v1 counted 23 such events (Success@30 ceiling 95.5 %); v2
excludes them, so the ceiling is 100 % with the default policy (every operating point reports
`success30_ceiling`; it drops below 1 only for k > 2). Later segments of a multi-segment clip can be eligible:
551 eligible30 events in 468 videos (349 videos have a first violent onset >= 30 s).
Trainers should use `TickLabels.valid[H]` / `hazard_target` and never re-derive labels.

## 4. Folds (`benchmark/folds_v2.json`)

* 5 grouped folds over all 7604 non-hold-out catalog videos (all roles, so trainers can also use other-anomaly and
  unlabelled videos without leakage); grouping key `split_group` (4630 groups); groups never cross folds (checked).
  `split_group` = catalog `group` (XD: source film / YouTube id; UCF, UBI: video id) merged across XD franchises
  (16 hand-listed franchises: Bourne, Bond, Mission Impossible, Love Death and Robots, Fast and Furious, ...) and
  near-duplicate pairs. v1 grouped by `group`: 41 near-duplicate pairs (4 violent) crossed folds, and the
  5 Bourne films sat in 4 folds.
* Stratified by `source_kind x {has eligible30 event, other violent, clean negative, other}`; a group's stratum is
  its most important video. Greedy balanced assignment by video count, then hours of footage in the stratum, then
  total count; ties broken by a salted sha256 of the group name (salt `va30-v2`): deterministic.
* Each fold f has a validation slice `val[f]` = about 1/8 of the training groups, group-disjoint from
  train(f) = all - test(f) - val(f). **Changed in v2:** inside each stratum the groups are ordered by a per-fold
  salted hash and the validation bin is `sha256(salt, f) % 8`. v1 always took bin 0 of a size-ordered assignment,
  so all five validation slices held the same large, long groups (pairwise overlap up to 81 %, validation clean
  clips 40 % longer than test). v2: maximum pairwise overlap 13 %, mean clean-negative duration 212 to 275 s in
  validation vs 251 to 317 s in test.
* `content_sha256` covers the assignment and validation lists; `Folds.load` refuses an edited file and the CLI
  refuses to overwrite an existing file without `--force`. `check()` also verifies that every validation video is
  in the assignment and not in its own test fold, and that no hold-out video is in the file.
* `Folds.role_in_fold(vid, f)` returns `None` for a video that is not in the fold file (hold-out, newer catalog rows,
  typos). Build training sets from `train_ids(f)` and filter catalog roles (`violent`, `clean_negative`, optionally
  `other_anomaly`; never `holdout`, `duplicate`, `contaminated`).
* **Decision (v2 rebuilt, not extended):** the extend path (`--base`) keeps every v1 assignment, so it cannot fix
  the near-duplicate leakage or the validation overlap. No model had been trained on v1 (only the zero-shot and
  label-free controls), so v2 was built from scratch with UBI-Fights included. Future catalog additions should
  extend v2 with `--base benchmark/folds_v2.json --out benchmark/folds_v3.json`.

## 5. Metrics (`anticipation/eval/va30.py`), in report order

Universe: fold videos with role `violent` (trusted onset sources; `--onset_sources` can restrict, e.g. to
`official`) or `clean_negative`. Out-of-fold: every video is scored by the model of its own test fold and all
threshold-free metrics pool the five test folds. Excluded ticks (section 3) never count anywhere.

1. **Far-window AUROC (headline frame metric).** Positives: unmasked **pre-violence** ticks with `30 < tte <= 60`
   (the first violent segment of a clip, before any annotated anomaly). Negatives (`clean`): unmasked ticks of
   clean negatives with >= 60 s of footage left. `secondary`: negatives = unmasked pre-violence ticks of any
   universe video with no onset within 120 s and >= 60 s left. Also reported:
   * `clean_time_matched`: clean negatives re-weighted so their **(source kind x time-since-start)** distribution
     equals the positives' (5 s bins up to 120 s, then 150, 180, 240, 300, 450, 600, 900, 1200, 1800, 3600 s;
     `TIME_MATCH_EDGES`); positives in strata without negatives are dropped. Any score that is a function of
     (source, time bin) gets exactly 0.50. `clean_time_matched_source_blind` is v1's version (time bins only).
   * `clean_uncensored`: without the 60 s right-censoring of clean negatives (23 % of clean clips are under 61 s and
     contribute nothing to `clean`; censoring inflates the position shortcut: `b_time_fit` 0.863 censored vs 0.840
     uncensored).
   * `clean_all_segments`, `secondary_all_segments`: the spec's literal definition (positives of every segment).
   **Decision (pre-violence headline), new in v2:** the spec counts every segment's lead-in, but 43 % of eligible
   events (and 44 % of far-window positive ticks) come after violence earlier in the same clip, while clean
   negatives never contain violence. A detector that only remembers past violence separates them without any
   anticipation (`b_memory`: 0.72 on `clean_all_segments`, 0.50 on the headline). Event-level metrics keep all
   eligible events (they are not fooled by memory, section 8).
2. **Per-horizon AUROC and AP**, H in 5, 10, 20, 30, 60: positives `y_H = 1` (unmasked, pre-violence); negatives =
   unmasked pre-violence ticks with no onset within 120 s and at least H s remaining (ticks with an onset in
   (H, 120] are excluded). Pooled, per source kind, (source x time) matched (`auroc_time_matched`), and the
   spec-literal `auroc_all_segments`. AP equals sklearn's (ties grouped).
3. **Event-level anticipation.** Alarm policy: raw alarm `score >= theta`; active if at least k = 2 of the last
   n = 3 ticks are raw alarms (causal); runs of active ticks separated by fewer than 10 s of silence are one
   episode; an episode starting less than 30 s after the previous counted alarm start is absorbed (refractory).
   **Changed in v2:** merging and the refractory clock restart at every event's `clear_start` (end of the previous
   segment's aftermath), so an alarm that detected event k cannot swallow a fresh anticipation alarm for event
   k + 1; an alarm that stays on across `clear_start` is not restarted (no free credit). For each eligible event the
   acceptance window is `[max(t0 - 120, clear_start), t0)`; the first alarm start in it gives `lead = t0 - start`.
   **Success@30 / @10 / @0** = fraction of eligible30 events with lead >= 30 / >= 10 / any alarm; lead
   distribution (median, p25, p75, mTTA = mean lead over alarmed events), uncapped; `success30_ceiling`. Also for
   the eligible60 subset (with Success@60) and for **`eligible30_late`**: events whose acceptance window starts at
   >= 30 s, so an alarm at the start of a clip can never count (254 events; Success@30 + Poisson reference).
   An alarm already running when the window opens does not count (no start in the window).
4. **False alarms per hour.** Alarm starts outside every `[t0 - 120, e + 10]` window and outside excluded spans.
   `fa_per_hour_clean` (primary) = starts in clean negatives / their unmasked hours; `fa_per_hour_all` = all
   universe videos, hours outside the windows; `fa_per_hour_clean_videos_under_1h`.
   `alarm_time_fraction_clean`: fraction of clean-negative ticks with an active alarm (FA/h counts a merged alarm
   once however long it lasts, so near-always-on operating points look cheap in FA/h alone).
5. **Operating points.** Per fold, theta is chosen on that fold's validation slice for validation FA/h (clean) of
   1, 2 and 5, then applied to the fold's test videos; outcomes are pooled. **Decision (threshold rule):** lower
   theta from +inf over the unique validation scores until validation FA/h first exceeds the target; keep the
   lowest theta before that. FA/h is not monotone in theta (once alarms merge into one episode per video, FA/h
   falls again), so a bottom-up search would select an always-on alarm. **Changed in v2:** the search is exact: a
   geometric top-down scan over the unique scores (1, 2, 4, ... below +inf) brackets the first crossing and
   bisection over the unique scores finds it; it equals brute force on every tested case (v1 used 5000 quantiles of
   all ticks and undershot the target by up to 0.15 FA/h). The AMOC-style curve (Success@30 vs test FA/h) sweeps one
   global threshold over pooled test scores (descriptive) and is plotted in `amoc.png` with both references.
   `--threshold_set nontest` (fold-agnostic score tables only) chooses thresholds on all non-test videos of each
   fold instead of the validation slice: legitimate for zero-shot scores (nothing was fitted) and about 4x more
   clean hours for the FA/h estimate.
6. **Chance references for Success@30.**
   * Poisson: `1 - exp(-FAR * W)` with FAR = test FA/h (clean) and W = 90 s: 4.9 % at 2 FA/h. Also
     `per_event_window` (the same formula over each event's actual window). It assumes alarm times independent of
     position in the clip, which VA30 violates (section 8.2).
   * **Position null (new in v2):** `position_null_success30` = the best Success@30 that position-only alarms
     reach at the operating point's test FA/h. Policies: oracle score families fitted on the evaluated ticks,
     P(tick in a Success@30 window), P(onset within 60 s) and P(onset within 120 s), each per (source kind x time
     bin) and per time bin alone, with 5 s and coarse bins; "alarm from time T on" (B-time) for all clips and per
     source kind; and "one alarm on ticks 1-3 of every clip of a subset of source kinds" (all subsets). Each family
     is swept over up to 50 global thresholds with the evaluator's alarm policy, and the null is the **upper concave
     hull** of all (FA/h, Success@30) points: a mixture of two position policies (for example a different one per
     fold, or one per random half of the videos) reaches any point between them, so the staircase envelope is not
     an upper bound. `position_null_success30_late` likewise for late events; the hulls are in
     `position_null_curve` and on the AMOC plot. Checked on the real benchmark: no operating point or AMOC point of
     `b_time`, `b_time_fit` or `b_time_source_fit` lies above it. A model anticipates only where it beats this null.
7. **Detection (never called anticipation):** per event (all violent events, eligible or not), any active alarm
   tick in `[max(t0 - 120, clear_start), e]`; and detection AUROC = in-violence ticks of violent videos vs unmasked
   ticks of clean negatives.
8. **Calibration** (only if all scores are in [0, 1]): Brier score for P(onset within 30 s) over unmasked,
   uncensored ticks (with the climatology Brier `p(1 - p)` for reference), ECE and a 10-bin reliability table
   (`reliability.png`).
9. **Uncertainty:** cluster bootstrap over test videos (`--cluster group` resamples catalog groups instead),
   1000 resamples, percentile 95 % CI, for every number including breakdowns and lead quantiles. **These CIs hold
   the thresholds fixed and do not resample the validation videos**, so they omit threshold-selection noise (one
   validation false alarm = 1 / `val_clean_negative_hours_per_fold` FA/h). `--threshold_bootstrap N` adds
   `threshold_aware_ci` per operating point: in each of N replicates the validation videos of every fold are
   resampled, theta is re-chosen with the same rule (on up to 300 candidate scores) and the test videos are
   resampled jointly. The baselines use N = 200. With several seeds every metric is computed per seed and
   `summary_over_seeds` holds the mean and standard deviation of the headline numbers.
10. **Breakdowns** at the 2 FA/h operating point and threshold-free: per source kind (`xd_movie`, `xd_youtube`,
    `ucf`, `ubi`, `yt`), interpersonal vs other violent classes (negatives = all clean negatives), audio present vs
    absent, **onset source** (`onset:official` / `onset:hivau` / `onset:ubi`, negatives = all clean negatives), and
    **event index** (`event:first_segment` / `event:later_segment`, event-level only); per-horizon AUROC/AP per
    source kind.

Implementation notes: AUROC is computed from a per-video pair-count matrix so every bootstrap resample is two matrix
products (equal to sklearn's Mann-Whitney AUROC, tested; weighted negatives for time matching); AP bootstrap uses
sparse per-video block counts. Event outcomes are vectorized (alarm starts, window membership, first start after
the window opens, detection via cumulative sums); only the refractory pass loops over episodes. One full evaluation
with 1000 resamples, breakdowns, the position null and N = 200 threshold-aware replicates takes about 1.5 to 2 min
on 16 CPU threads.

## 6. Score table contract and CLI

One row per scored tick (parquet or csv): `video_id` (catalog id), `t_sec` (ticks 1..floor(duration)), `score`
(higher = violence more likely soon; may only use media in [0, t_sec]), `fold` (the model that produced it;
each fold's model must score its test fold AND its validation slice; `-1` = fold-agnostic, used for every fold),
`seed` (optional, default 0). **Errors (v2):** duplicate `(seed, fold, video_id, t_sec)` keys, NaN or non-integer
`fold` / `seed`, NaN / inf `score` or `t_sec`, and a `(seed, video)` with both fold -1 rows and fold-specific
rows (v1 silently kept the last duplicate, turned NaN folds into -1 and fell back to -1 rows). Missing ticks are
an error unless `--allow_missing` (sample-and-hold for gaps, global minimum before the first score, missing videos
dropped and reported) or `--restrict_to_scored` (evaluate only the universe videos present in the table; reported
as such and not comparable with full-universe numbers). Extra ticks beyond a video's duration are ignored.

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m anticipation.eval.va30 --scores runs/x/scores.parquet --out_dir benchmark/results/x \
    [--name x] [--folds benchmark/folds_v2.json] [--n_boot 1000] [--k 2 --n 3] [--fa_targets 1,2,5] \
    [--cluster video|group] [--onset_sources official,hivau,ubi] [--restrict_to_scored] [--allow_missing] \
    [--no_breakdowns] [--no_position_null] [--threshold_set val|nontest] [--threshold_bootstrap 200] \
    [--meta run_config.json]   # feature set, extraction config, model config, train command (BASELINE 6.6)
```

Python: `from anticipation.eval.va30 import Benchmark, EvalConfig, evaluate, save_results`;
`res = evaluate(df, Benchmark.load(), EvalConfig())`. `metrics.json` records the fold file name and content hash,
videos.csv / events.json hashes, scores file hash, git commit (read from `.git` without running git), command line,
config, threshold rule, threshold set and CI note, coverage, and all metrics. `events_op2.csv` lists every event
with its index, window start, lead, detection and onset source at the 2 FA/h operating point (for audits).

### Capped clean negatives

Feature extraction cuts clean negatives longer than 300 s to their first 300 s (`benchmark/extract_manifest_v1.csv`:
`duration_sec` is the capped length, `orig_duration_sec` the full one; 655 of 6,231 videos), so their scores stop at
tick 300 while `videos.csv` still lists hours of footage. `--horizons` evaluates such videos only up to the cap:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m anticipation.eval.va30 --scores runs/x/scores.parquet --out_dir benchmark/results/x \
    --horizons benchmark/extract_manifest_v1.csv
```

Python: `Benchmark.load(horizons=path)`, `bench.with_horizons(path_or_dict)` or `EvalConfig(horizons_csv=path)`.

* The csv needs `video_id` and `duration_sec` (other columns are ignored). A listed video whose duration is more than
  1 ms shorter than its `videos.csv` duration is evaluated as a video of that length: ticks 1..floor(horizon), no
  scores required after it, and score rows after it are dropped before the table is validated (they cannot raise
  or move `global_min` / the probability check). Unlisted videos and videos listed at their catalog duration or longer
  keep the catalog duration.
* Only clean negatives may be shortened. A violent, hold-out or any other role that a horizon would shorten raises
  a `ValueError` naming the videos (they were not capped, so a short horizon would hide their onsets).
* Everything is computed as for a natively short video: FA/h hours, `alarm_time_fraction_clean`, excluded spans
  (clipped at the horizon) and the right-censoring of videos without violence, so far-window negatives need 60 s of
  footage left and horizon-H negatives H s (the last 60 s of a 300 s cap are in no far-window negative set), while an
  alarm in the last seconds still counts as a false alarm. `fa_per_hour_clean_videos_under_1h` uses the evaluated
  length, so capped videos are in it.
* Results are comparable only between runs with the same horizons file: capped validation negatives also change the
  threshold selection.
* `metrics.json` gets `benchmark.video_horizons` (file, sha256, rows listed, shortened, hours catalog vs used),
  `counts.video_horizons` (test clean negatives shortened, `clean_negative_hours_used`, `clean_negative_hours_catalog`)
  and `coverage.score_rows_beyond_horizon_ignored`; `report.md` states it. Without the flag none of these keys exist
  and the numbers are identical to before (`tests/test_va30_horizons.py`).

## 8. Findings and recommendations

1. **Position + provenance shortcut (frame level).** Onsets sit early in their clips while clean negatives are
   long, and the footage source is visible in every frame. `b_time_source_fit` (no pixels, no labels at test time)
   reaches far AUROC 0.86 and AUROC@30 0.90. Any model with elapsed-time information (recurrent state, positional
   encodings, history length) or start-of-video artifacts can pick this up. **Recommended amendment to BASELINE
   6.5:** report `far_window_auroc.clean_time_matched` (source x time) next to the headline, and require a new model
   to beat `b_time_source_fit` on the headline with non-overlapping CIs AND to be clearly above 0.5 on the matched
   AUROC.
2. **Position at the event level (v1's claim was wrong).** An alarm raised at the start of every clip of a source
   anticipates every onset between 32 and 122 s, and pooled FA/h makes alarms in a source with few clean hours
   cheap. The Poisson reference assumes alarm times independent of position and is beaten twice over by
   `b_time_source_fit`. **Recommended amendment to BASELINE 6.4.6:** Success@30 must beat `position_null_success30`
   at the same FA/h (CI lower bound above it, threshold-aware CI where thresholds are noisy); also report
   `eligible30_late`, whose acceptance window excludes the clip start (its null is lower, about 7 % at 2 FA/h).
   The reviewer's label-free exploit (one alarm on ticks 1-3 of a set of clips), re-run on v2: 40 % of XD movie
   clips -> 7.4 % [5.5, 9.6] at 1.49 FA/h, Poisson 3.7 %, position null 7.1 % (the CI contains the null); all XD
   movie clips -> 17.1 % at 3.56 FA/h, Poisson 8.5 %, null 17.1 %; all UCF clips -> 11.6 % at 3.03 FA/h, null
   14.5 %; all UBI clips -> 6.9 % at 2.48 FA/h, null 11.9 %. Late-window Success@30 is 0 in every case.
3. **Detection memory.** 239 of 551 eligible events follow earlier violence in the same clip. On the spec-literal
   far AUROC a perfect detector with memory scores 0.72 without anticipating anything. The headline now uses
   pre-violence ticks; event-level metrics are unaffected (a memory alarm that stays on never starts inside a later
   window; alarms do not merge across `clear_start`). **Recommended amendment to BASELINE 6.4.1 / 6.4.2:** count
   only pre-violence ticks, report the all-segment versions as secondary, and add `b_memory` to the controls.
4. **Ground truth.** 374 of 551 eligible events rest on unvalidated HIVAU train onsets, annotated in a different
   style from the official ones; the one independent comparison differs by 11 s and a quick look at 8 spot-check
   sheets found the incident already under way at 3 of the marked onsets. The teacher does three times better on
   official-onset events than on HIVAU-onset events (20.0 % vs 6.5 %), although that comparison is confounded with
   the source (official = XD test + UCF test; HIVAU events in the teacher subset are mostly UCF train). **Recommended:**
   complete the human spot-check (`anticipation.data.spotcheck`, 50 HIVAU + 30 official sheets ready) and treat
   `onset:official` as co-primary until then.
5. **Eligible-event count** grew to 551 (468 videos), about 110 per test fold; a Success@30 near 10 % has a 95 % CI
   of about +-2.5 points on the full universe (+-4 on the 198-event teacher subset), and threshold-selection noise
   adds to that (threshold-aware CIs).
6. **Thresholds near the always-on regime** must be read with `alarm_time_fraction_clean` (b_time at "2 FA/h" keeps
   an alarm on for 47 % of clean time).

## 9. Gotchas and limitations

* The evaluator requires a score for every tick of every universe video in every fold's test and validation set;
  build score tables from `bench.labels(vid).t_sec` (`io.tick_times`).
* Use `labels_for_video` (or `Benchmark.labels`) for training labels: it applies the excluded spans. Reading only
  `segments` from events.json misses mixed-label `other_segments` and the cross-clip masks.
* An eligible event's acceptance window starts at the previous segment's aftermath end (or at the end of an excluded
  span), so alarms raised during or right after a previous fight never count as anticipation of the next one.
* XD multi-segment clips are often edited compilations: the "lead-in" before a later segment may be a different
  scene. The headline frame metrics use first segments only; `event:first_segment` gives the event-level view.
* HIVAU onsets differ in style from the official ones and are unvalidated (2.5); `--onset_sources official`
  reproduces the official-only universe and every result has an `onset:official` breakdown.
* `has_audio` decodes every track once (cached). UCF `audio_stream` is true for 1597 videos but only 490 carry
  non-silent audio; UBI has no audio at all.
* The catalog only reads XD train files listed in the downloader's `verified.jsonl` and UBI files listed in
  `.extracted.jsonl` (in-flight downloads are skipped).
* `folds_v2.json` stores the videos.csv hash at creation; later catalog rebuilds are noted in `metrics.json`
  (`benchmark.note`); fold membership always comes from the fold file.
* The curated YouTube hold-out (`role = holdout`) is never in a fold; score it separately once human onsets exist.
* `b_teacher` scores are discrete (0, 0.1, 0.5, 0.8, 0.9, ...), so many ticks tie; AUROC treats ties as 0.5 and
  coarse scores can make some FA/h targets unreachable (theta = +inf, never alarms).
* Near-duplicate detection is a lower bound (2.2), and the franchise list is hand-made for the 102 XD movie groups.
